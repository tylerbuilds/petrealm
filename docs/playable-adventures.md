# From clicking through to playing

The intended audience is roughly **8–18**. Player feedback exposed two problems: the first version mostly advanced text, and the first direct-control trails rewarded walking without demanding much skill. The trails now require timed catches, with misses that can end an attempt.

## What you can do now

Choose a Rillum and go straight into Broadroot Rush. Move with **WASD or arrow keys**, press **Space to dash**, or use the touch movement pad and Dash button. Tapping a clear destination also moves your companion along a navigable route.

Approach a glowseed to open its timing challenge. Stop the moving marker inside the target with **Space** or the **Stop** button. The smaller centre earns a perfect catch. Stopping outside the target or letting the challenge expire costs a chance. **Three misses end the run**, with no rewards from that attempt. Retry immediately for free.

Catch at least five of the eight seeds, then reach the neighbour at the glowing arch. Moving brambles, mud and pulsing ripples briefly slow the companion; dashing avoids them. The run clock counts up, including time spent catching seeds. It never ends the run by itself.

| Trail | Play | First completion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Broadroot Rush | Learn the catch timing while weaving through fallen roots. | Fern arch; opens Brook Skip. |
| Brook Skip | Faster markers and tighter targets, with crossings between water channels. | Skipping stones; opens Lantern Trail. |
| Lantern Trail | The fastest markers and narrowest targets, with ripples along the shore. | Golden petals. |

Each trail awards a star for finishing, another for all eight seeds and another for beating its target time. Secrets and personal bests persist. Gentler timing widens the targets, slows the marker and eases hazards, with the same rewards and progression. It still requires a successful stop.

## Why come back?

The immediate loop is **move → time a catch → dodge → finish → build**. Seeds successfully caught and brought to the finish pay for moss, reeds and a pebble pool. First-completion rewards are free and appear at home automatically. Built decorations can be swapped without paying again. Replaying lets you improve your route, earn missing stars or find a secret.

The three home locations are the roots, canopy and riverside. Earned objects appear in the home scene; they are not just an entry in an inventory. Stories, discovered finds and the quieter walks remain available, but completing their dialogue is not required to play.

## Pauses, saves and old worlds

Switching to Home or another in-app page pauses and preserves the current in-memory run. Return to Play and choose **Keep exploring**. Pausing, leaving the browser tab and opening Save stop the clock, timing marker and challenge countdown. Restarting a trail is free. Reloading the page starts an unfinished trail again; completed discoveries and home changes are saved.

A run receives a saved identifier before play. Its result can be collected once, even if another tab tries to collect it. A replacement world cannot receive a previous world's result. Failed storage writes keep the current visit exportable. Explicit save errors retain the result and offer another save attempt.

Version 1 Under the Hedge saves migrate to version 2 by adding empty play progress. Existing names, appearances, stories, finds, neighbours and prepared walks are retained. The original stored bytes remain until the next successful save. Importing still retains the previous world as a backup. The separate previous Petrealm game remains available.

## What this revision establishes

This responds to the reported lack of challenge with mandatory timing, limited mistakes, increasing difficulty and immediate retries. Walking or tapping a route alone cannot win. It is not evidence that all children and teenagers will enjoy it equally. The next useful playtest is whether players can start unaided, choose to retry a trail and use an earned reward at home. A broad 8–18 audience needs observation across ages; automated checks establish functionality, not enjoyment.

There are three fixed course layouts, with moving obstacles. This is not a procedural open world. No online accounts, multiplayer, purchases, advertising, tracking or external runtime services were added.
