# A companion worth coming back to

Research translation, 10 October 2026. This note describes the companion experience update; it is not a retention study or a claim of feature parity with another game.

## Source and confidence

The supplied [Research on virtual pets](https://app.notion.com/p/Research-on-virtual-pets-3f5e2a7d47da806faae3f1b71f39c899) was read through the connected Notion fetch tool. The response contained 78,172 characters and reported its native verification state as **unverified**. The executive synthesis, recommended Expedition Companion concept and validation plan informed this update.

The research distinguishes documented mechanics, academic findings, press reports, player accounts and interpretation. It explicitly says that its small, mostly self-reported studies do not establish what causes retention. Its author did not play the comparison games. The recommendations below are therefore product hypotheses. We have not independently reverified every external citation, and no regulatory or commercial claim from the report is used as a release requirement.

## Wins already present

Petrealm already had nine original companions, recognisable illustrated places, authored three-choice trails, keepsakes to display at home and optional visible growth. Those are useful foundations for identity and shared history. They remain original Petrealm work.

The browser-native implementation, local saves, validated imports and recovery downloads protect continuity without an account or a running remote service. Two minigames already share a daily currency cap. Fixed NPC prices avoid a competitive player economy. There is no player trading, chat, telemetry or product network dependency to operate.

## Research translated into behaviour

| Research hypothesis | Implemented response | What still needs player evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Responsive behaviour and shared history can support attachment. | Care, practice, trail decisions and expedition encouragement build three bounded tendencies: curiosity, kindness and courage. The dominant tendency changes the companion's greeting and biography. Authored return stories refer to species and shared activities. | Players describe their friend's behaviour and recognise it without relying only on appearance. |
| Short visits can offer a meaningful choice and a satisfying return. | Our journal contains a free outing planner with three places and three encouragements. The world's first outing takes 30 seconds; later routes take two, five or ten minutes. The selected encouragement and rotating authored scene shape the story and keepsake. Returns never expire, and no currency or supply payment is required. | Players read and recall the story instead of treating the return as another reward button. |
| Returning after an absence should feel safe. | Elapsed time no longer drains Fullness, Happiness or Energy. Seven completed quest days earn the extra gift without requiring consecutive days. Existing active-play costs remain explicit. | People return after a break without describing guilt, obligation or a need to catch up. |
| A visible history can help players choose their own goals. | A dedicated journal combines biography, recent return stories and keepsakes, with links to the existing trail collection and meadow display. The home screen puts the companion's next story before secondary systems. | Players choose a place, encouragement or find because it interests them, and can explain how their friend changes. |
| Portability is part of protecting an attachment. | The journal exports an original SVG portrait and an illustrated offline HTML story that can be opened or printed. World save export and recovery remain available. | Players understand the difference between a keepsake export and a restorable world save. |
| Gentler interaction should accommodate different ways of playing. | Memory meadow starts without a time limit, with an optional timed round. Keyboard focus restoration, card announcements and Space-key handling have been improved. | Keyboard and assistive-technology participants can finish the important journeys comfortably. This is not a full accessibility certification. |

## Deliberate limits

The new tendencies are authored, deterministic game state, not an autonomous language model. They influence greetings and biography; the chosen encouragement drives the expedition scene. We should not claim that all outcomes adapt to learned personality.

The save keeps the **latest three written expedition memories per companion**, the completed outing total and the collected keepsakes. It is a bounded recent journal, not an unlimited archive. Players can export a story to keep a particular edition. Existing saves receive the new companionship fields while keeping their earlier pets, care, money, outfits and trail history. The world remains on the validated version 3 format with an explicitly versioned optional companionship extension.

The update adapts the report to the existing product. It retains optional NPC matches, practice, shops and banking. It does not add a player market, social service, monetisation, notifications or analytics. Trail finds and expedition keepsakes retain their own provenance in the save, and either can be chosen for the meadow's three display places.

This note supersedes older research comparisons wherever they describe time-based care decay or a consecutive-day quest reset as current Petrealm behaviour. Historical comparison JSON and release receipts remain dated evidence of their own builds.

## Validation still to do

Automated state and browser checks can establish functional behaviour, save recovery and keyboard continuity. They cannot show affection, fun or lasting voluntary return. Current implementation checks belong in [verification](../verification.md); older release results are not evidence for this update.

For the next small, consent-based playtest, ask participants to adopt, encourage one outing, read its return, find a favourite journal entry and take a break. Ask them to describe the companion, predict what an action changes and explain their next self-chosen goal. Revisit after a gap without reminders. Compare observations with the previous build, record confusion and reports of boredom or guilt alongside positive reactions, and treat the small sample as direction rather than proof. No tracking or participant outreach is introduced by this implementation.
